# EVERDUNE GENESIS

## The Hook

**The World Remembers.**

Everdune is a quiet frontier where an ancient living force called the Hearthsong once connected places, people and nature through memory.

After an event known as **The Quieting**, that memory faded.

The player arrives in Larkmere Valley and gradually discovers that the world can remember again.

Repairing, restoring, exploring, befriending and discovering can awaken **Echoes**: memories left behind by places, people, creatures and objects.

This is the foundation of the game identity.

## Player fantasy

The player is a **wayfarer**, not a chosen one.

They may fish, farm, craft, build, explore, fight, collect, study, decorate, befriend or simply wander.

Quiet play is a first-class playstyle.

## Opening mystery

The player arrives because of an old travelling letter promising them a home.

The sender is apparently dead.

The house is gone.

A sealed stone marker remains, carrying the player's family name.

## Magic

Magic is expressed through Echoes rather than conventional spell lists.

An Echo is a memory anchored to the world.

Examples:
- a bridge remembers the people who built it
- a forest remembers a forgotten path
- an abandoned home remembers its family
- a sword remembers its owner
- a poisoned spring remembers its cause

## Tone

Quiet wonder, warmth, mystery, humour, melancholy without misery, adventure without constant urgency.

## Canon deepening — 2026-09-28

### Hearthsong

Hearthsong is not a god, ruler or conventional spell source. It is a natural resonance created by repeated acts of care, belonging and memory: paths walked often, bridges repaired, orchards tended, homes lived in, rivers fished, songs shared and promises kept.

A place becomes easier for the world to remember when people keep showing up for it.

### The Quieting

Three centuries ago, several communities attempted to centralise Hearthsong so its memory could be stored, measured and directed. The attempt failed.

Hearthsong did not die. It fragmented.

The surviving fragments became Echoes: local memories that can still be heard when the right person, object or place is approached with enough attention.

The historical wound is therefore about control versus relationship, not a simple good-versus-evil war.

### The Wayfarer family

The player's family belonged to a loose tradition of Wayfarers sometimes called **Listeners**. They did not command Hearthsong. They learned to notice it and leave places stronger than they found them.

The family disappeared from Larkmere generations ago.

The player is not prophesied to save the valley. The old family marker matters because the house, road and people connected to that family still carry memories that have not finished resolving.

### The letter

The travelling letter is genuine. It was written by the player's last known relative before their death, inviting the player to inherit a home and a life in Larkmere.

The house is gone because it was deliberately dismantled after the last Wayfarer left.

The sealed marker was left behind as a promise: someone from the family may return when they are ready to listen.

It is not a prophecy.

### The long mystery

Restoring Hearthsong exactly as it was would repeat the mistake that caused the Quieting.

The healthier future is a distributed Hearthsong: many small memories rooted in communities, homes, forests, rivers and relationships rather than one central authority.

The player's actions matter at two levels:
- immediate: the valley becomes more useful, beautiful or connected;
- historical: the valley becomes capable of remembering itself again.

### Faction truths

**Hearthkeepers** believe tradition protects memory. They are right that memory can be lost; they are wrong when preservation becomes ownership.

**Wayfinders** believe roads and connections keep communities alive. They are right about movement; they underestimate how much memory lives in relationships.

**Ashen Circle** believe concentrated Hearthsong was dangerous. They are right about the danger of control; they are wrong if they conclude that memory itself should be extinguished.

**Greenwardens** believe the land remembers without humans. They are right that nature carries memory independently; they are wrong if they dismiss the value of human care.

No faction is secretly the one correct answer.

### Restoration rule

A restoration is never just a quest completion flag.

Repairing a bridge can create a shorter route, change NPC routines, reveal a new gathering spot and make an old Echo accessible.

Tending the Glass Orchard can change what grows there, who visits and which memories can be heard.

Building the player's home creates a physical record of their choices.

Helping a person creates memories that can surface later in dialogue.

**Progress should leave evidence in the world.**

### End-state question

The eventual question is not simply whether the player defeated a final enemy.

It is:

**What kind of memory did you help this place become?**

The answer should be visible in the valley, the player's home, NPC relationships, discovered Echoes and the ordinary activities the player chose to spend their time doing.
